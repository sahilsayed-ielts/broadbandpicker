**Version:** 2.0 — June 2026  
**Goal:** Make BroadbandPicker the most trusted, most comprehensive broadband resource in the UK  
**Revenue model:** Awin affiliate commissions (£50–£120 per sign-up) + future display

## Strategic Vision

Most broadband comparison sites are thin deal aggregators. They exist to funnel users to affiliate links as fast as possible. They do not genuinely help people understand broadband.

**BroadbandPicker's position:** The only UK broadband site that explains everything — technology, rights, pricing, providers, availability — without dumbing it down or hiding the commercial relationship. People come to learn, stay to compare, and leave through an affiliate link with full confidence in their choice.

This is how Uswitch built authority from 2000 onwards. This is the model that earns Awin approval, Google trust, and returning users.

## Part 1: Awin Approval — What They Actually Check

Awin reviews every publisher application manually. When they look at BroadbandPicker they will check six things:

### 1. Site completeness
Every page must have real, non-placeholder content. No "coming soon" sections. No broken links. No lorem ipsum. The site must look finished and operational.

**Required before applying:**
- All 12 provider pages fully written with real data
- All guide pages with complete, accurate text
- Homepage presenting a clear, professional value proposition
- /about page with a genuine explanation of who runs the site and why it exists
- /contact page with a real email address or contact form

### 2. Affiliate disclosure
This is a Awin requirement AND an Advertising Standards Authority (ASA) legal requirement in the UK. Awin will reject sites that do not clearly disclose commercial relationships.

**Required before applying:**
- A sitewide footer notice: "BroadbandPicker may earn a commission when you click provider links. This does not affect our editorial independence or the deals we show."
- A dedicated `/how-we-make-money` page (see Part 2 below)
- Affiliate links in articles must be labelled or disclosed inline where material

### 3. Editorial independence and methodology
Virgin Media explicitly states they want publishers with "up-to-date and exciting content relevant to their audience." BT and Sky want content aligned with their brand guidelines. This means the site must demonstrate it has a real editorial process, not just a link farm.

**Required before applying:**
- A `/how-we-review-broadband` page explaining the methodology
- Provider reviews that mention negatives as well as positives (Awin and individual advertisers actively check this — a site that gives every provider 5 stars is an instant red flag)
- Clear "last reviewed" dates on all content

### 4. Traffic signals
Awin states they usually do not approve publishers without "a website with decent traffic or a social following." The site will not have organic traffic immediately after launch. Mitigate this by:
- Creating a Twitter/X account and LinkedIn page for BroadbandPicker before applying
- Submitting the sitemap to Google Search Console immediately
- Publishing all content before applying — 80+ indexed pages signals a real site
- Optionally: writing a guest post or getting one mention from any broadband/tech publication before applying

### 5. Privacy policy and legal compliance
Required: full GDPR-compliant privacy policy, cookie consent banner, terms of use. These already exist on the site. Verify they reference email collection (the sign-up forms) and analytics (GA4) specifically.

### 6. Professional presentation
The site must look like a real business. Awin reviewers will judge within 10 seconds. Check: no broken images, mobile-responsive layout, fast load times (Core Web Vitals must pass), professional logo, consistent branding.

## Part 2: Trust Infrastructure — Pages That Must Exist

These are not "content" in the editorial sense. They are trust signals that Awin, Google, and users all look for. None of these exist yet. They must be built before content publishing begins.

---

### Page: `/how-we-make-money`

**Title:** How BroadbandPicker Makes Money

**What it covers:**
- We earn a commission from providers when a visitor clicks through and signs up
- The commission we earn does not influence which providers we show or how we rank them
- We compare deals from providers whether or not they have an active affiliate relationship with us
- Our comparison tables are ordered by value (speed per pound, or user-selected criteria) not by commission rate
- Specific disclosure: "BroadbandPicker is a trading name of [legal entity]. We are registered in England and Wales."

**Why it matters for Awin:** Shows transparency. Individual advertisers like BT and Sky read this page. A publisher that hides the commercial relationship will be rejected or have accounts suspended.

**Why it matters for Google:** Google's E-E-A-T guidelines explicitly penalise sites with hidden commercial intent. This page is what separates a trusted affiliate from a spam site.

---

### Page: `/how-we-review-broadband`

**Title:** How We Review and Compare Broadband Deals

**What it covers:**
- Data sources: Ofcom Connected Nations reports, provider published prices, live API feeds, Trustpilot, Ofcom complaints data
- Review criteria: monthly cost, contract length, download speed, upload speed, latency, setup cost, customer service rating, Ofcom complaints per 100,000 customers, out-of-contract price (the trap most sites ignore)
- How we update: deal tables are reviewed monthly; provider reviews are updated whenever Ofcom publishes new data or providers change their pricing
- Independence: "We accept no payment for editorial coverage or for placement in our guides. The rankings in this article are determined by our methodology, not by commercial relationships."
- Author disclosure: who wrote this review, their background

**Why it matters:** Uswitch publishes a 20,000-person customer satisfaction survey. Which? runs lab tests. BroadbandPicker does not have that scale yet — but this methodology page signals seriousness and earns Google's trust. It also answers the question Awin's compliance team will ask: "how does this site produce its recommendations?"

## Part 5: Authority Content — What Separates a Resource From a Directory

The following content types are what Uswitch, Which?, and MoneySavingExpert use to build authority that comparison tables alone cannot create. BroadbandPicker needs versions of each.

### The Broadband Glossary
See Part 2. 50+ terms. Published as a single long page with anchor links. Target: any user who searches "[term] broadband meaning" lands here.

### The Speed Calculator Tool
A page with an interactive calculator: user selects number of people in household, activities they do online, and the tool recommends a minimum and recommended speed. At the end, a CTA: "Find deals that match this speed at your postcode."

This does not require a complex web app — it can be built as a JavaScript-powered form on a standard page. It becomes a reference resource that earns backlinks from tech journalists and broadband help forums.

### Original Research and Data
Once per quarter (every 3 months), publish one piece of original data:
- A survey of BroadbandPicker users on a broadband topic ("How many UK households don't know they could be on a cheaper social tariff?")
- An analysis of Ofcom's publicly available data presented in a readable format
- A cost comparison: "We tracked broadband prices for 6 months — here is what we found"

Original data earns backlinks. Backlinks build domain authority. Domain authority makes all other pages rank higher.

### News Coverage
A `/news` section with 2–4 articles per month covering:
- Provider price rise announcements
- Ofcom decisions and reports
- New providers launching or expanding
- Infrastructure news (Openreach full fibre rollout milestones)
- Government broadband policy (voucher schemes, USO updates)

News content has two purposes: (1) freshness signals for Google, and (2) being the first credible result when someone searches a breaking broadband story.

## Part 8: E-E-A-T Signals — What Google Needs to See

Google's E-E-A-T framework (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trust) was expanded in December 2025. Broadband content is adjacent to financial decisions (YMYL). Here is exactly what the site needs:

### Experience
- First-person language where appropriate: "We tested..." / "We compared..." / "In our analysis of Ofcom data..."
- The methodology page must show the work, not just state the conclusion
- Speed test screenshots or data tables where possible

### Expertise
- Every article must have a named author or reviewer byline
- Even if Sahil is the only person involved, a brief bio on the About page ("Sahil has followed the UK broadband market for X years") satisfies this requirement
- Link to Ofcom, Uswitch studies, EY consumer research as sources — do not just assert facts
- "Last reviewed: [date]" on every article

### Authoritativeness
- Get mentioned by one external publication before applying to Awin — even a small tech blog or a post in a broadband forum like ISPreview.co.uk or thinkbroadband.com
- The original research pieces (Part 5) are the main mechanism for earning backlinks and third-party citations
- Cite Ofcom data directly and link to source documents

### Trust
- `/how-we-make-money` page (see Part 2)
- `/editorial-policy` page
- Privacy policy ✅ exists
- Cookie consent ✅ should exist
- HTTPS ✅ (Vercel provides this)
- No deceptive patterns — do not present paid placements as organic editorial
- Prices accurate at time of publication with a "verified on [date]" note

## Part 9: Content Format Rules

**Comparison tables:** Every commercial-intent page must have a comparison table within the first 300 words. Columns: Provider, Monthly cost, Speed, Contract, Key feature, CTA. Updated monthly with a date stamp.

**People Also Ask boxes:** End every guide with 4–6 FAQ entries in Q&A format. Google's PAA feature increased 34.7% in UK results between Feb 2024 and Jan 2025. Answer questions in 40–60 words — long enough to be useful, short enough to be featured.

**Speed boxes:** Any page mentioning speed should include the speed reference table from Pillar 3 above, or a version relevant to the specific use case.

**Postcode checker CTA:** Every availability or area-related page must prompt the postcode check. This is the primary conversion tool on the site.

**Affiliate CTAs:** Every provider mention in a guide should link to that provider's Awin affiliate URL. Minimum: at least one CTA per 400-word section. Do not cluster all CTAs at the bottom.

**Pricing accuracy:** Every article with pricing must include: "Prices verified [month year]. Offers may change — check current deals using our comparison tool." This protects against dated content misleading readers and satisfies Awin's advertiser requirements on accuracy.

**British English throughout:** The site is for a UK audience. "£" not "$". "Fibre" not "fiber". "Authorised" not "authorized". "Colour" not "color". Mobile, not cell. Contract, not plan.
