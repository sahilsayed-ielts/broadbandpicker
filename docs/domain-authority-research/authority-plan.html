BroadbandPicker Authority Plan

Google already trusts our content enough to show it about 400,000 times a quarter. What it doesn't have yet is evidence that other people trust us: almost nobody links to us, searches for us by name or comes back. This plan covers how the UK broadband leaders built that trust, what we can copy cheaply, and the quick wins to do first.

Why domain authority is the bottleneck

Moz DA and Ahrefs DR are third-party scores based mostly on how many trusted sites link to you. Google uses its own version of the same idea. With 2 referral visits in a quarter and no branded demand, our authority is close to zero. That's why our big “best X deals” guides sit at an average position of 45: Google ranks money pages from MoneySuperMarket, Uswitch and MSE first because many sites link to them.

Where we already win

Pages with less competition from big brands already rank: provider-vs-provider pages average position 13.8, the satisfaction research page 6.2, postcode pages 17.8. ChatGPT already recommends us, sending 139 visits. Engagement is high too (86% engaged sessions, about 3 minutes per visit). The content is good enough. What it lacks is outside endorsement.

Linkable tools and data · months 1 to 6

Build things people bookmark, share and cite

Ranked by how likely each is to earn links, return visits and AI citations for the effort involved. Each one reuses data or code we already have.

Build by Dec 2026

A. 2027 broadband price-rise checker

The user picks a provider, enters their monthly price and contract start date, and sees the exact £ rise due in April 2027 and what they would pay over the rest of the contract. They then see cheaper deals with no mid-contract rise (we already have that guide and the structured price-rise data). Rises are now set in pounds and pence under Ofcom's 2025 rules, so the maths is exact.

Why: Uswitch and Which? both run one, and the press covers price rises every January to April. A results card shared on WhatsApp (“my bill goes up £36 in April”) brings people back to the page. A companion “Price rise tracker 2027” table is something journalists cite. Timing matters: it has to be live and indexed before January.

Month 1–2

B. Full fibre league table from Ofcom open data

Ofcom's Connected Nations data is free under the Open Government Licence and includes local authority and constituency files. We rank every council area and constituency on full fibre and gigabit coverage and on change since last year, publish one page per area, and link those into our postcode hubs.

Why: this is the Cable.co.uk model scaled to the UK. Local papers reliably cover “our town ranks 280th of 361”, MPs share constituency rankings, and each area page is a natural link target that strengthens the postcode section. Repeat it every spring and winter when Ofcom updates the data.

Month 2

C. Speed test 2.0 with shareable results, then a quarterly speed report

Restore the speed test first. Then add a results card (“You got 72 Mbps. Typical on your plan: 67 Mbps”), a “Is this normal for my provider and area?” comparison, and anonymous logging of postcode district, ISP and result. Once we have enough tests, publish a quarterly “Fastest broadband providers and towns” report.

Why: SpeedGeo gets ISPreview coverage this way. We completed 105 tests in 90 days, which is too few to publish. A report needs thousands per quarter, so it comes after the shareable version grows usage. Until then, B gives us the data story.

Month 2

D. Monthly UK Broadband Price Index

Our deal sync (deals:sync) already records prices. We keep a monthly snapshot of the cheapest deal per speed tier (100, 500, 900 Mbps, gigabit), plus averages, and publish a chart and short commentary on the first working day of each month.

Why: nobody else publishes a simple public time series like this. Cost-of-living reporters need exactly this number. Every month we re-pitch it, and AI assistants cite pages with dated statistics.

Month 2–3

E. Social tariff and help-with-bills finder

Answer three or four questions (benefits received, postcode, needs) and see every social tariff you qualify for, with price, speed and how to apply. Printable for advisers.

Why: this makes the resource outreach in quick win 9 much stronger. Councils and charities link to tools far more readily than to articles. It also matches Ofcom's push on social tariff awareness.

Month 3

F. Contract-end reminder and deal alerts by email

“Email me 30 days before my contract ends, with the best deals then.” Build it on the newsletter system that is already coded (Supabase double opt-in, unsubscribe). Add price-drop emails for deals people have already saved.

Why: this is the MSE pattern. It is the most direct fix for our 11% return rate and brings people back at the moment they are ready to switch, which is when they click affiliate links.

Month 3–4

G. Landline switch-off and telecare checker

Tells households (especially older people and carers) whether their alarm, landline phone or fax is affected by the move to digital phone lines, what to do, and what it costs. Builds on the guides currently offline.

Why: the deadline drives search demand, and Age UK branches, councils and carers' groups link to useful explainers.

Month 4–6

H. Annual “BroadbandPicker Awards” from our own data

Categories such as best value full fibre, fewest price rises and best customer service, based on our satisfaction research, price index and reviews, with the methodology published. Winners get a badge.

Why: Uswitch's accreditation shows providers announce awards and link to them (YouFibre published its own news story about it). We need 6+ months of index data first so the awards are credible.

Later

I. Embeddable widgets

A “Check full fibre in your area” or speed test widget for community groups, estate agents and local news sites, with a plain branded credit link.

Google treats keyword-stuffed widget links as unnatural, so the credit must be branded and we accept nofollow. The aim is reach and brand searches, not link juice.

AI assistants

ChatGPT is already our second-biggest channel

143 visits (16%) came from AI assistants, nearly all from ChatGPT, with 92% engaged. AI assistants prefer to cite pages with original, dated numbers and clear methodology, which is exactly what assets A to E produce. Each data page should open with a two-sentence summary that contains the key figure and date, include a methodology section, and use Dataset structured data (already used on the research pages). Keep the AI Overview check from the page-build rules on every new asset.

What we will not do

Shortcuts that would damage the site

Buying links or guest posts. UK digital PR links cost hundreds of pounds each for a reason. Sold links on link farms are what Google's spam updates (March and June 2026) target, and a penalty would wipe out the affiliate income.

Thousands of near-identical pages. About 2,360 postcode district pages are thin templates. The separate postcode plan sets them to noindex while keeping them live. Thin pages at scale drag down how Google rates the whole domain.

Fake reviews or star ratings. We already refuse to add review schema we can't back up. Keep it that way.

Publishing on other sites to borrow their authority. Google's site reputation abuse policy targets affiliate deal pages placed on news and other trusted domains.
