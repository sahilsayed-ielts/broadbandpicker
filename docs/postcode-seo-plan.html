BroadbandPicker · SEO plan · 15 October 2026

Postcode pages: fewer, better, local

BroadbandPicker publishes 2,832 postcode pages. About 2,770 of them are the same 505-word template with different Ofcom percentages. Google indexes them today, but its guidance on scaled content says this is the kind of inventory that can drag down a whole site. This plan keeps every page that earns clicks, makes those pages genuinely local, and folds the rest into 121 postcode-area hubs.

1 · Decision

Keep 470 postcode pages in Google, consolidate about 2,360

A correction first: postcode pages are not missing from the sitemap. They are in a second sitemap, /postcode/sitemap.xml, which robots.txt points to. It lists 2,832 pages, and Google has indexed every one we sampled.

The real issue is quality, not discovery. 96% of the site's indexable URLs are postcode pages, and the auto-generated ones share 93% of their text. They have no place names, use July 2024 coverage data and show the same 12 national deals. Google's spam policy calls this pattern "scaled content". Today it isn't hurting rankings: these pages sit at a median position of 5.2 for rare exact-postcode searches. But Google's helpful-content signal is applied site-wide, so it's a standing risk to the pages that matter, such as the guides.

The plan: keep and properly localise the 410 district pages that earn demand, plus the 60 city and curated pages. Build 121 postcode-area hubs. Keep the other ~2,360 district pages working for visitors, but tell Google not to index them, and remove them from the sitemap. All 214 clicks those pages earned in 90 days come from pages we keep.

What a district page is today

  • Identical template: AB10, TQ2 and WS1 are all exactly 505 words. Only the percentages change.
  • No place names: AB10 is central Aberdeen, but the page never says so. Searches that reach these pages include "broadband deals Pershore", "Kirkcaldy", "Hornsea" and "Dymock".
  • Old data: Ofcom coverage dated July 2024, and "prices verified 21 June 2026".
  • The same 12 national deals everywhere, not filtered to networks that reach the district.
  • Barely linked: the /postcode hub links to 15 postcode pages. The other ~2,800 are found mainly through the sitemap.
  • But they convert: 32 provider clicks per 100 visits, among the best on the site, because postcode searchers are ready to buy.

Search Console's sitemap count is stale

At its last read (13 October), Search Console counted 60 URLs in the postcode sitemap against 2,832 live. Resubmitting the new, smaller sitemaps fixes the reporting and gives us a clean "indexed vs submitted" figure per group.

City pages earn no money

The 10 hand-written city pages had 11,673 impressions and 41 visits, but zero provider clicks in 90 days. They rank around position 31 for competitive terms like "broadband London". The same deals-first problem as the guides.

3 · What Google wants

The rules that decide this

Spam policy

Scaled content abuse

Google's spam policies (March 2024) target "many pages generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users", however they're made. Swapping a postcode and a few numbers into one template is the textbook example.

Spam policy

Doorway pages

Google names "pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" as doorways. District pages that all show the same national deals fit that description. Local, filtered results don't.

Ranking systems

Helpfulness is judged site-wide

Google says a site with "relatively high amounts of unhelpful content" can see other content perform worse, and that removing unhelpful content can help the rest. With 96% of URLs being thin postcode pages, this is the main risk.

Sitemaps

List only pages you want indexed

A sitemap should contain canonical, indexable pages, with lastmod changed only when the content really changes. Pages marked noindex shouldn't be in it.

Indexing

Use noindex, not robots.txt

To keep a page usable but out of results, use a noindex robots meta tag and leave it crawlable. Blocking it in robots.txt stops Google from seeing the noindex. Merge truly duplicate pages with a 301 redirect.

Site structure

Link to what matters

Pages Google can only find through a sitemap look unimportant. A clear hub → area → district structure with descriptive links tells Google which pages carry weight.

Crawl budget isn't a concern here: Google says it matters mainly for sites with a million-plus URLs. The concern is quality, not crawling.

Why noindex rather than delete or redirect

The site's own postcode checker sends visitors to these URLs, and they convert at 32 clicks per 100 visits. Deleting them would break that journey. Redirecting would send a WM1 visitor to a page about a different district. Noindex keeps the visitor experience, removes the quality risk, and can be reversed per page.

Self-correcting rule

A monthly job reruns the audit. A noindexed district that starts earning clicks or visits is promoted: localised, indexed and added to the sitemap. An indexed district with no impressions for 6 months is reviewed.

Quality gate before indexing

A district page only becomes indexable if scripts/audit_postcode_pages.py measures at least 40% unique text (today: 7%), a real place name, and coverage data under 12 months old. Pages that fail stay noindexed until they pass. Any claim that appears only in an AI Overview needs a primary source first, as with every page build.

6 · City pages

Apply the deals-first lessons to the 10 city pages

City pages get the most impressions per page (London alone: 3,287), but rank around position 31 and produced no provider clicks. They're built on the same article-first template as the guides were.

  • Use the deals-first layout from the TV-guide pilot: verdict, postcode check and deals in the first screen.
  • Link down to every area hub and indexed district in the city (London → E, N, SE, SW, W … hubs).
  • Local evidence: networks building in the city, borough-level coverage, dated.
  • Wait for the TV-guide pilot's 28-day result before rolling out, so we copy what works.

8 · Roadmap

Phased, measured, reversible

Week 1

Data foundations

  • Add ONS post town and local authority for every district; refresh Ofcom coverage to the latest release.
  • Build the "keep list" from Search Console and GA4 (≥10 impressions or a click, plus all curated).
Weeks 2–3

Area hubs and localised district template

  • Build the 121 area hubs and the localised district template; link hub → areas → districts.
  • Run the quality gate: only passing pages are indexable.

Consolidate

  • Record the change for before/after measurement (7 / 28 / 56 / 90 days).
Weeks 4–6

City pages

Monthly

Keep it self-correcting

  • Rerun the audit; promote districts that start earning demand; review indexed pages with none.

10 · Your decisions

Three things to approve

Decision 1

Approve the noindex of ~2,360 pages

Pages stay live for visitors. Reversible per page. This is the step that addresses the quality risk.

Decision 2

The threshold

Recommended: ≥10 impressions or a click (410 pages). Stricter: ≥20 (239 pages). Looser: ≥5 (875 pages, more localising work).

Decision 3

Order of work

Recommended: phases 1–3 first (data, hubs, consolidation), city pages after the TV-guide pilot result in mid-November.

Method. scripts/audit_postcode_pages.py classified all 2,832 URLs in /postcode/sitemap.xml, pulled 90 days of Search Console data per URL, inspected 60 URLs with Google's URL Inspection API (10 city, 25 curated, 25 district: all "Submitted and indexed"), and measured visible word count and the share of 5-word phrases unique to each page. GA4 sessions and events cover 17 Jul – 14 Oct 2026. Full data: docs/postcode-page-audit.json, docs/postcode-ga4-by-class.json, docs/postcode-district-gsc-90d.json.

Limits. Search Console hides rare queries, so most district impressions can't be tied to a search term. The unique-text measure is a proxy for duplication, not a Google score. The index-status sample is 60 of 2,832 pages. Google's guidance is quoted from its spam policies, ranking-systems guide, sitemap and noindex documentation as of 2026.
