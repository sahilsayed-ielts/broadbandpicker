# SEO/GEO strategy — SerpApi competitive research + GSC ground truth

_Compiled 2026-09-01. Two real data sources, not assumptions: 35 live SerpApi
Google-UK queries (`docs/geo-serp-research/2026-09-01-raw.json`) and Google
Search Console's actual last-28-days performance
(2026-08-02 to 2026-08-29, via `scripts/check_search_console_connection.py`'s
credentials)._

## The headline finding changes the brief

The request was "what content should we build to rank and get cited by AI."
The real, more useful answer from the data: **the site's biggest problem right
now is not a content gap, it's that the few pages already getting the most
Google attention rank too low to be clicked.** Building more broad content on
top of that would repeat the same mistake at a larger scale.

### Finding 1 — zero presence on every head-term query tested

Ran 35 real UK broadband queries through SerpApi's Google engine (deals,
switching, technical explainers, affordability, provider comparisons,
altnets, tools, local intent). Result:

- **BroadbandPicker: 0/35 in organic top 10. 0/35 cited in an AI Overview.**
- 33/35 queries had a Google AI Overview present — so AI Overviews are firmly
  live for this category, BroadbandPicker just isn't in any of them.
- Domains actually occupying organic top 10 across these 35 queries: uswitch.com
  (24 appearances), moneysupermarket.com (17), moneysavingexpert.com (16),
  virginmedia.com (15), comparethemarket.com (10), gocompare.com (9),
  broadband.co.uk (7), ofcom.org.uk (7), broadbandchoices.co.uk (5).

This is a genuinely hard competitive set — long-established comparison sites
and finance publishers with large backlink profiles built over years. No
on-page content fix alone beats that for the broadest head terms
("cheap broadband deals UK", "best broadband provider UK").

### Finding 2 — the site IS indexed and DOES get real impressions, just not where you'd want them

`site:broadbandpicker.co.uk` returns roughly 2,300 indexed pages (mostly the
postcode-district pages), so indexing isn't the problem. Search Console for
the last 28 days: **85,159 impressions, 88 clicks — a 0.1% click-through
rate.** That gap is the real story. Broken down by page:

| Page | Impressions | Clicks | Avg. position |
|---|---|---|---|
| `/guides/best-broadband-and-tv-deals` | **23,809** | 16 | **63.0** |
| `/guides/best-full-fibre-broadband-uk` | 7,174 | 3 | 50.6 |
| `/` (homepage) | 3,400 | 6 | 32.3 |
| `/guides/best-business-broadband-providers-uk` | 2,963 | 1 | **80.2** |

Three pages account for **~40% of all impressions on the entire site**, and
all three sit at an average position where almost nobody scrolls (page 4-8).
That's an enormous amount of Google's attention being wasted. Separately,
"5g broadband" / "5g broadband uk" / "5g broadband deals" queries together
pull in ~139 impressions at position 39-68 with **zero clicks** — pointing at
`best-5g-home-broadband-uk`, the guide Google is associating with those
queries.

### Finding 3 — the site already knows how to rank; it just hasn't been pointed at winnable fights

Buried in the same GSC data, several *narrower* pages already sit on page 1:

| Page | Avg. position |
|---|---|
| `/providers/compare/ee-vs-talktalk` | **7.0** |
| `/guides/starlink-vs-fibre-broadband-uk` | **6.2** |
| `/postcode/nw1` | **6.1** |
| `/providers/cuckoo` | **10.4** |
| `/postcode/gu1` | **11.8** |
| `/postcode/bn1` | **11.8** |

These are exactly the page types this session has already been building —
provider-vs-provider comparisons and genuinely local postcode content — and
they're the only page types in the whole dataset actually winning against the
big aggregators. That's not a coincidence: a two-provider comparison or a
single postcode district is a much narrower fight than "cheap broadband deals
UK," and it's one a smaller, more specific site can win.

## What this means for priority, in order

### P0 — Fix the pages already burning the impression budget

Before building anything new, strengthen the four pages above (position
32-80, high impressions, near-zero clicks): deeper comparison tables, fresher
Ofcom/price data, stronger internal linking from the homepage and other
guides, FAQ schema if missing, and a hard look at whether the title/meta
target is realistic (a guide titled to compete head-on with uswitch for "best
broadband and TV deals" may need a narrower, still-valuable angle it can
actually win, alongside the broad one). This is the single highest-leverage
fix available: the demand already exists and is already being shown the
page, it just isn't converting.

### P1 — Double down on the proven pattern: comparisons and postcode pages

Keep building provider-vs-provider comparisons and genuinely local postcode
content (the M1/NG1/RG1/CT1/RM1/S17/S8 work already done or planned this
session) ahead of more broad "best of" guides. This is the format with actual
evidence of winning page-1 positions against this specific competitive set.

### P2 — New GEO-shaped content for AI Overview citation

Lower-competition, definitional queries are the realistic route to an AI
Overview citation, since AI Overviews favour clear, structured, well-sourced
answers over broad commercial pages. Two concrete candidates surfaced by this
research:

- **"What is an altnet broadband provider?"** — real query, has an AI
  Overview, BroadbandPicker has no dedicated explainer. Comparison sites have
  a natural authority angle here that uswitch/MSE don't specifically own.
- **Wi-Fi 7 explainer** — already identified and tracked from the Connect
  Fibre content plan (`content-wifi7-broadband-explained-guide`); this
  research independently confirms the same gap from a different angle.
