# Instagram feed posts (professional publisher grid)

Feed is the shopfront. Educational carousels and one brand tile. If it looks like a voucher or a meme page, it does not go here.

Carousels: slide 1 is the claim, slides 2-4 earn the save, last slide is the URL.

Caption formula: two-line hook with keywords, short explanation, save this, link in bio, 3-5 hashtags.

Paste-ready blocks with ChatGPT prompts: `professional-content.md`.

### IG-006 — Fibre versus full fibre

- **Grid:** 2,3 · **Format:** carousel
- **Hook:** If the package does not say full fibre or FTTP, copper may still be in the mix.

**Slides**
  - Fibre versus full fibre
  - Fibre on an advert often means FTTC: copper from the cabinet.
  - Full fibre / FTTP is glass to the premises.
  - Upload and evening speed usually hold up better on full fibre.
  - Check the technology at your postcode.

**Caption (paste)**

```
If the package does not say full fibre or FTTP, copper may still be in the mix.

A lot of UK advertising still says fibre when the last stretch is copper from a cabinet (FTTC). Full fibre, or FTTP, is glass all the way to the premises.

It is usually stronger on upload and less likely to fall apart at 7pm. If a package does not say full fibre or FTTP, assume copper may still be in the mix.

Independent comparison. Rankings are not sold.

Save this if you are reviewing your broadband this month.

Link in bio
https://broadbandpicker.co.uk/guides/fttp-vs-fttc-explained?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=ig-006

#UKBroadband #FullFibre #BroadbandDeals #FTTP
```

**First comment:** FTTC is fibre to the cabinet. FTTP is glass all the way to the premises. The words on the advert are not the same thing.

**Alt text:** BroadbandPicker carousel. Fibre versus full fibre, FTTC versus FTTP.
