Deals-first guide redesign

Guides are the most-seen page type on BroadbandPicker, but they earn almost nothing. It also sets out the authority work that design alone cannot do.

1 · Decision

Redesign the commercial guide template first

Guides have more Google impressions than every other page type combined (99,508 vs 84,968), and 84% of those impressions land on 23 "best X deals" roundup pages. Those pages are written as articles, but people searching "broadband and TV deals" expect a page of deals. Google ranks the deal pages of MoneySuperMarket, Uswitch and MoneySavingExpert for these searches and puts ours around position 40–80.

The fix is a deals-first template: verdict, postcode check and filterable deal cards at the top, with our evidence-led advice underneath. That is the layout every leading UK broadband affiliate uses. Explainer guides (how-tos, "what is Digital Voice") keep a lighter article layout.

Need 1 · from TV queries + PAA

"Is bundling actually cheaper?"

"How much should I pay for TV and broadband?", "Is it cheaper to get broadband and TV together?", "best TV and broadband deals with sports". They want totals, not headline prices.

Need 2 · from phone queries + PAA

"Keep my landline, pay less"

"unlimited phone calls and broadband packages", "deals for pensioners", "fibre without a landline". Often older readers, so legibility and plain words matter.

Need 3 · from fibre queries + PAA

"Is full fibre worth it, and can I get it?"

"Is full fibre better than normal broadband?", "What broadband can I get?", "What is the disadvantage of fibre?". Availability is the first question.

Need 4 · from "best" queries

"Just tell me who's best for me"

"best broadband uk", "best fibre broadband", "who is the best Wi-Fi provider". They want a clear pick per need, with reasons.

Need 5 · trust (PAA)

"Who says so?"

"Which deals are best according to Martin Lewis?" Readers look for a named, trusted person behind the recommendation. We currently have none.

Need 6 · contract terms

"No nasty surprises"

"monthly broadband", "no contract", mid-contract rises. Smaller (3%) but these readers convert well (3 clicks from 2.9% of impressions).

These needs are inferred from the search terms and People Also Ask questions, not from surveys. Section 9 adds an on-page feedback loop to test them.

5 · Prototype

See it working: the deals-first prototype

The new template applied to our biggest guide. It keeps every trust feature we already have, moves the deals to where readers expect them, and adds the authority signals we lack.

/guides/best-broadband-and-tv-deals
Interactive prototype

Best Broadband and TV Deals, rebuilt deals-first

A working version of our biggest guide (25,700 impressions in 28 days), built with the six real packages from data/tvBroadbandBundles.ts and the page's current verdict, Ofcom figures and FAQs. Nothing is invented. The author name is a placeholder until you choose one.

Open the prototype

Tip: press Show design notes at the top of the prototype to see the evidence behind each part. Try it on your phone too.

What to try

1Tap Live sport. The list narrows to sport bundles, and the ones that match get a green outline.
2Sort by Lowest total cost. Each card adds up 24 months including confirmed rises. Virgin Media's £74.99 bundle comes to £1,975.26.
3Change the Start month. EE's totals shift because its rise lands every 31 March.
4Save a deal. The count in the header and phone bar updates, as the live shortlist does.
5Enter a postcode. The prototype explains what the live check will do; it doesn't pretend to check.
6On a phone, scroll past the deals. A single bottom bar appears and hides again when deals are on screen.

Module rules

①Title with month and year, as MoneySuperMarket and CompareTheMarket do. Change the month only when prices are actually rechecked; never bump dates automatically.
③Verdict: one pick per need from Section 3, each with its reason. Reuses CiteableAnswer.
④Postcode in the first screen. After a check, cards re-sort to what is available. Reuses PostcodeContextBar.
⑤Deal cards show the total contract cost and the price rise in pounds, the information our audience asks about most. Reuses DealsClient filters and SaveDealButton. Where a rise isn't confirmed (Sky), the card says so instead of guessing.
⑦Keep all current advice, reordered and lightly restructured. No deleting content during the pilot, so we isolate the effect of layout.
⑩Named author with credentials and Person schema. Needs a real person, which is your decision (see Section 7).
⑫One sticky action, never two. No pop-ups, fake countdowns or "only 2 left" claims.

6 · Becoming a top broadband site

Authority is what moves page 5 to page 1

Layout fixes the mismatch with what searchers want. These six moves build the trust Google needs before it ranks a newer site above MoneySuperMarket.

People

Named experts

One or two real, named editors with bio pages, LinkedIn and a "why trust me" line. Every commercial guide is signed and dated. This matches Which? and the 50–66% of affiliate pages that do it.

Data

BroadbandPicker Price Index

A monthly report on the average UK broadband + TV price, the mid-contract rise, and the cheapest full fibre per Mbps. Built from data we already track (Awin and provider feeds). Cable.co.uk shows original data earns links every year.

Structure

Hub and spoke

Three hubs (TV bundles, phone + broadband, full fibre), each linking down to audience guides, provider pages and postcode pages, with links back up. One page per search intent, no duplicates.

Freshness

Visible price checks

"Prices checked [date] by [name]" on every card, driven by the provider deal agent. Stale deals hide automatically.

Reach

Digital PR

Pitch Price Index findings to the consumer press (money desks, ISPreview, regional news). Each story is a backlink to a hub.

AI answers

Quotable, sourced verdicts

0 citations in AI Overviews so far. Short verdicts with a named source, a date and an Ofcom link are what AI answers quote. Keep the AI Overview check rule for every build.

7 · Missing from the brief

Things the plan has to cover that weren't asked for

Two top guides are offline right now

/guides/broadband-and-mobile-bundle-discounts-uk (8,954 impressions, 13 clicks) and /guides/cheapest-way-to-watch-sky-sports-uk (5,433 impressions, 43 clicks, our best-clicking guide) return "page not found" on the live site. They exist only in uncommitted local code. Deploying them is the fastest win in this plan.

Guides don't earn money yet

1.4 provider clicks per 100 visits, against 12.7–29.5 elsewhere. The redesign is judged on revenue per visit, not only on looks.

A real named author is needed

The biggest authority gap needs a real person: you, or a hired broadband writer. We must not invent one. This is your decision.

Pages may compete with each other

/deals, /compare and the "best X deals" guides target overlapping searches. Before rollout, give each search intent one page and merge or redirect the rest.

UK rules for price claims

Since January 2025, Ofcom requires mid-contract rises to be shown in pounds and pence. The DMCC Act bans fake reviews, and ASA/CMA rules require clear affiliate disclosure. Cards must show total cost and rises honestly.

Small samples

Guides had 292 visits in 28 days. Pilot results need 6–8 weeks and should be read as direction, not proof.

Speed and accessibility are part of UX

Deal cards and filters must not slow the page: keep Core Web Vitals green, readable at 200% zoom and usable with a keyboard. Older landline readers benefit most.

Revenue tracking per page

Connect Awin conversions to the guide that sent them (click reference per page), so we can see which template earns the most, not just which gets clicks.

8 · New ideas

Features that could set BroadbandPicker apart

None of the 44 scraped affiliate pages asked "was this helpful?", and only 2 cited original data of their own. These ideas fill that space.

Calculator · P1

True cost over the contract

One slider: "How long will you stay?" Each card's total updates, including setup, the April rise and the out-of-contract price. It answers "how much should I pay?" directly.

Tool · P1

"Am I overpaying?" check

Enter your current monthly bill and provider. We show how far above the cheapest available deal you are, and the yearly saving. It captures the out-of-contract crowd.

Personalisation · P1

Three-question matcher

What do you watch? How many people stream? Need a landline? Cards re-sort and "Best for you" chips appear. No sign-up required.

Retention · P2

Contract-end reminder

"Remind me 4 weeks before my contract ends." This builds a list that returns every 18–24 months, the most valuable audience in broadband.

Sharing · P2

Share my shortlist on WhatsApp

Saved deals become a link the household can open, without logging in. Builds on the existing /saved-deals.

Data · P2

Price history per deal

A small line chart: "lowest price in 90 days". It shows readers whether today's offer is genuinely good. Needs the deal agent to keep a price history.

Trust · P1

"Was this helpful?" + "What's missing?"

Two taps at the end of each guide, sent to GA4. A free, permanent research loop on the audience needs above.

Local · P2

Altnet spotlight after postcode

When a postcode has an altnet (Community Fibre, Hyperoptic and others), show it as "Local full fibre option". Big sites often bury these, and many altnets are Awin advertisers.

Later · P3

Ask-the-guide assistant

Answers eligibility questions ("can I get this in a flat?") only from our own checked data and sources. Pilot after the template proves itself.

9 · Roadmap

Pilot on three pages, measure, then roll out

Week 0

Review the prototype and quick fixes

  • You review the prototype and approve or change it.
  • Deploy the two missing guides (bundle discounts, Sky Sports).
  • Add month and year to the titles of commercial guides whose prices have been rechecked.
  • Decide on the named author (your decision).
Weeks 1–3

Build the components

  • CommercialGuideShell: turn the approved prototype into real site components (verdict, trust strip, postcode, deal cards with filters, sort and total cost, sticky panel).
  • AuthorBox + Person schema, jargon tooltips, true-cost slider, "was this helpful?".
  • Reuse DealsClient, SaveDealButton, PostcodeContextBar, CiteableAnswer, OnThisPageNav.
Week 4

Pilot pages

  • /guides/best-broadband-and-tv-deals: 25,700 impressions, position 43.6
  • /guides/best-phone-and-broadband-deals: 13,671 impressions, position 52.6
  • /guides/best-full-fibre-broadband-uk: 6,058 impressions, position 31.6
  • Run a live AI Overview check per page before publishing (standing rule).
Weeks 5–12

Measure and start authority work

  • Compare 6 weeks after launch with 6 weeks before, allowing for the 3-day Search Console delay.
  • Publish the first BroadbandPicker Price Index and pitch it to the press.
  • Build the three hubs and clear up overlapping pages.
Week 12+

Roll out

  • Apply variant A to the other 20 commercial roundups, then variant B to explainers.
  • Keep only the modules that improved provider clicks without hurting rankings.
