## Real competitive landscape (new finding)

Connect Fibre is not alone in this exact geography. Live research surfaced direct
regional overlaps worth knowing before writing any comparison content:

- **BeFibre** — an altnet with coverage explicitly spanning Lincolnshire,
  Northamptonshire, Nottinghamshire, Staffordshire and Warwickshire — the closest
  direct regional rival found.
- **Connexin** (Hull-based) — holds a Project Gigabit contract to upgrade premises
  across Nottinghamshire and West Lincolnshire.
- **County Broadband** — full-fibre altnet across Essex, Norfolk, Suffolk and
  Cambridgeshire, overlapping Connect Fibre's Essex and Cambridgeshire villages
  directly.
- **CityFibre** — holds Project Gigabit delivery contracts spanning Warwickshire
  and Northamptonshire (wholesale, not a direct consumer rival, but relevant
  context for "who else is building fibre here" content).

Source: live search across gocompare.com, uswitch.com, fibre-systems.com and
broadbandswitch.uk, 2026-08-30.

## Content clusters and page types

### 1. Evergreen guide: "Is Wi-Fi 7 broadband worth it?" — **proceed now**

Real, standing search demand: BroadbandDeals.co.uk, CompareFibre and
broadbandswitch.uk all already publish "Wi-Fi 6 vs Wi-Fi 7" / "is Wi-Fi 7 worth it"
guides, confirming genuine query volume rather than a speculative topic.
BroadbandPicker doesn't have an equivalent guide today.

- **Primary keyword cluster**: "is wifi 7 worth it", "wifi 7 vs wifi 6", "wifi 7
  broadband explained", "wifi 7 router UK"
- **Angle**: honest, technical-but-plain explainer (4K-QAM, lower latency, when it
  actually matters — 15+ devices, gigabit+ plans, VR/competitive gaming — vs. when
  Wi-Fi 6 is still fine). Cite YouFibre (Wi-Fi 7 standard across its range) as an
  existing example; add Connect Fibre once/if their affiliate terms are resolved,
  or reference them neutrally without a tracked link in the meantime.
- **GEO angle**: structure as a direct-answer opening paragraph ("Wi-Fi 7 is worth
  it if... it isn't necessary if...") plus an FAQ block — the exact shape that gets
  lifted into AI Overviews and chat-assistant answers, and matches this site's
  existing FAQ-schema pattern.
- No affiliate link dependency — can be written and published immediately.

### 2. Update existing "UK broadband social tariffs" guide — **proceed now**

## Brand voice and asset notes (from their Mini Brand Guidelines PDF)

Connect Fibre uploaded `marketing-materials/connect-fibre---mini-brand-guidelines-v5.1.pdf`.
Useful for accurately representing them in any content that quotes or features
the brand directly:

- **Strapline**: "faster. fairer. flawless." — lowercase, full stops, exactly as
  supplied. Don't retype it in a different font/case if it's ever reproduced.
- **Logo**: primary/secondary/tertiary lockups with strict minimum sizes and clear
  space rules; black logo on light backgrounds, yellow on dark. Available for the
  provider page once built.
- **Brand colours**: CF Yellow `#FFD200`, CF Black `#1B2021`, Community Orange
  `#FF860B`, Rural Green `#04A777`, Royal Fibre purple `#4B006E` (Magenta is
  retired — don't use it in anything new).
- **Their own copy rules** (for accurately quoting them, not a mandate for
  BroadbandPicker's own house style): sentence case headlines, no full stops on
  headlines/bullets, left-aligned copy, contractions, active voice, numbers zero
  to nine spelled out and 10+ as digits, "Wi-Fi" exactly (never WiFi/wifi/WIFI),
  and "1000Mbps" with no space before the unit. Worth knowing if we ever embed a
  direct quote or screenshot of their marketing copy, though BroadbandPicker's
  own editorial content should keep its own established style (e.g. "1,000 Mbps"
  with the space, consistent with the rest of the site).
