# Awin negotiation playbook

Reference facts for `analyse-terms`, `recommend` and `draft-reply` to negotiate
from — sourced, not invented. Update this file if you find better evidence;
don't let the scripts quietly drift from what's actually true here.

## Commission benchmarks (UK broadband comparison sites)

- Comparison sites typically earn **£50–£120 per sign-up (CPA)** from
  mainstream ISPs. Smaller altnets typically pay less than large national
  providers, reflecting lower customer lifetime value and smaller marketing
  budgets.
- Broadbandchoices' own public affiliate programme pays **up to £30 per
  sale**, 30-day cookie — useful as a *floor* reference, not a target.
- MoneySuperMarket offers **bespoke CPA rates** to high-value partner types
  (cashback, loyalty, voucher) and **exclusive rates in exchange for extra
  exposure** — confirms that the headline rate card is a starting point, not
  the ceiling.
- Source: [Netify: UK broadband reseller programmes](https://netify.co.uk/insights/broadband-reseller-companies/), [broadbandchoices affiliate programme](https://www.broadbandchoices.co.uk/solutions-for-affiliates), [Awin: MoneySupermarket Broadband programme](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/25756).

## How to actually negotiate a better rate (Awin's own advertiser-side guidance)

- Advertisers raise commission for partners who can demonstrate **consistent
  performance** or offer **additional, relevant exposure** in return —
  negotiation is explicitly framed as an exposure-for-rate trade, not a
  one-sided ask.
- Ask to see the advertiser's **media pack** — most have one outlining what
  exposure they'll pay extra for (dedicated content, newsletter placement,
  homepage feature, social).
- **Performance-based increases** (a trial at a higher rate, reviewed after a
  set period) are a standard, lower-risk way to open a rate conversation —
  propose a trial rather than demanding a permanent increase up front.
- **Exclusive placements or codes** are specifically called out as valuable
  to advertisers — offering an exclusive Connect Fibre placement or code in
  exchange for a better rate is a legitimate, expected ask.
- Ask specifically for **"Commission Flexibility"** / to speak with an
  account manager about customised terms — this is Awin's own named
  mechanism for tailored rates, not a special favour.
- Source: [Awin: How to build strong partnerships through smart negotiation](https://help.awin.com/advertisers/docs/en/how-to-build-strong-partnerships-through-smart-negotiation), [Awin: Best practices for setting commission rates](https://help.awin.com/advertisers/docs/how-to-approach-commission-rates-for-your-program-1), [Awin: Commission Flexibility](https://www.awin.com/us/how-to-use-awin/commission-flexibility).

## Deduplication — what's actually enforceable

- Awin requires advertisers to **publish their de-duplication policy** in
  their programme T&Cs (so demanding to see it in writing is a reasonable,
  standard ask, not a special concession).
- Advertisers must give **adequate notice before changing** a de-duplication
  policy, specifying which channels are affected and why — worth getting
  this notice commitment confirmed explicitly if it isn't already.
- Per Awin's own rules: **"Commission rates may not be less than the
  equivalent commission offered by the advertiser on other affiliate
  marketing networks or in-house programs."** This is real, quotable
  leverage — if Connect Fibre pays other channels more, they're not allowed
  to lowball an Awin partner below that.
- Source: [Awin: De-duplication](https://help.awin.com/docs/de-duplication), [Affiliate Window De-Duplication Guide (PDF)](https://d2yb7bnhemvcvl.cloudfront.net/blog.affiliatewindow.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/11/De-duplication-Guide.pdf).

## General negotiation posture

- Affiliates gain leverage by demonstrating **consistent performance over
  time** — a brand-new relationship has less negotiating power than a proven
  one, so it's reasonable to accept modest initial terms and revisit them
  after a performance track record exists, rather than holding out for
  everything up front.
- Source: [Affiverse: Negotiating Terms and Conditions — An Affiliate's Guide to a Win-Win Partnership](https://www.affiversemedia.com/negotiating-terms-and-conditions-an-affiliates-guide-to-a-win-win-partnership/).

## How this applies to BroadbandPicker specifically

BroadbandPicker's core product (postcode-level comparison, provider deal
tables) is exactly a "price comparison site" — the channel type advertisers
most often deduplicate against. Before accepting any invitation:

1. Get the **commission rate and model** in writing — never accept "superb
   pricing" as a substitute for a number.
2. Get the **de-duplication policy** in writing, specifically how it treats
   price-comparison-site referrals vs. the advertiser's own site and other
   partners.
3. If the disclosed rate looks low against the £50–£120 CPA benchmark above,
   it's reasonable to ask for a trial period at a higher rate, or propose an
   exclusive placement (a dedicated review, a featured comparison-table slot)
   in exchange.
